Città Studi

Milan's university quarter: a 1920s campus, student bars, a vast open-air pool and the birthplace of the Negroni Sbagliato.

  • East of the centre
  • Student life
  • Piola (M2)
  • Home of the Negroni Sbagliato

The neighbourhood

Città Studi, the 'city of studies', is named after the university campus at its heart. The Politecnico di Milano, founded in 1863, opened its new buildings on Piazza Leonardo da Vinci in 1927, alongside the science faculties of the University of Milan, and the name spread to the whole district that grew up around them.

It isn't a sightseeing district, and that's part of its charm: tree-lined residential streets, student cafés and trattorias, and a slice of everyday Milan. The Politecnico is Italy's leading technical university, and the Nobel chemist Giulio Natta and the architects Renzo Piano and Aldo Rossi all studied there.

Just to the north is Casoretto, an old village with a 15th-century abbey church and the pizzeria on this list.

    Bar BassoThe bar where the Negroni Sbagliato was invented, by reaching for sparkling wine instead of gin. Order one in the giant glass. It has long been the meeting place for artists and designers.

Città Studi at a glance

Where
East of the centre, around Piazza Leonardo da Vinci and the Politecnico
Getting there
Piola (M2) for the Politecnico, with Loreto (M1, M2) and Lambrate FS (M2) on the edges
Best time
Evenings for Bar Basso and the brewery; in summer, the open-air Piscina Romano (June to August)
Known for
Universities, student life and the Negroni Sbagliato
Name
'City of studies', after the university campus built here in the 1920s
