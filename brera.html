Brera

Milan's old artists' quarter: cobbled lanes, a world-class gallery and the city's prettiest evening stroll.

  • Centro storico
  • Best on foot
  • Lanza (M2)
  • Market: 3rd Sunday

The neighbourhood

Brera is the old artists' quarter on the northern edge of the historic centre, a short walk from La Scala theatre. Its name comes from the Lombard word braida, a grassy clearing outside the city walls.

The students and painters turned it into Milan's Montmartre, and the bohemian streak survives in the cobbled lanes, galleries and late-night bars. Today it's also one of the city's smartest addresses, full of independent boutiques and design showrooms, and during Milan Design Week the Brera Design District is where much of the action happens. Very nice for a fancy daytime walk.

It's small enough to see on foot in an afternoon, and it's at its best in the early evening, when the lanes fill up for aperitivo.

Brera at a glance

Where
Just north of the Duomo, between the Castello Sforzesco and Via Manzoni
Getting there
Lanza (M2) or Montenapoleone (M3), with Moscova (M2) for the northern end. About 15 minutes on foot from the Duomo.
Best time
Late afternoon into aperitivo, or the third Sunday of the month (not August), when antiques and craft stalls fill Via Fiori Chiari
Known for
Art, antiques, design and evening strolls
Name
From the Lombard braida, a grassy clearing just outside the old walls
