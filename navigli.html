Neighbourhood guide

Navigli

Milan's canal quarter: towpaths, old washhouses and the city's liveliest aperitivo hour.

  • South-west of the centre
  • Best at sunset
  • Porta Genova (M2)
  • Antiques market: last Sunday

The neighbourhood

Navigli is named after its canals, the navigli, which once linked Milan to the Ticino river and Lake Maggiore. Work on the Naviglio Grande began around 1177, and by 1211 it reached the city gates and in 1509 Leonardo da Vinci finished work on one of its locks.

Most of the city's canals were covered over in the 1930s, but the Naviglio Grande and the Naviglio Pavese survive, meeting at the Darsena, Milan's old harbour. The last cargo barge arrived in 1979. Today the towpaths are lined with bars, trattorias, vintage shops and artists' studios.

Come in the late afternoon, walk the Naviglio Grande as the light goes, and stay for aperitivo. Weekends are busy; weekdays are calmer.

What to do

The numbers match the map.

  1. 1
    The Darsena and the Naviglio GrandeStart at the Darsena, Milan's old harbour, built in 1603 where the two canals meet and restored for Expo 2015. Then walk the Naviglio Grande towpath: off it is the Vicolo dei Lavandai, where the neighbourhood's laundry was scrubbed on stone slabs until the 1950s, and on the last Sunday of the month (earlier in December) antiques stalls line the canal.Map for The Darsena and the Naviglio Grande (opens in a new tab)
  2. 2
    Basilica di Sant'EustorgioFounded in the 4th century to hold relics of the Three Kings, most of which Frederick Barbarossa carried off in the 12th century. Their Roman sarcophagus is still here, and the Portinari Chapel has frescoes by Vincenzo Foppa.Map for Basilica di Sant'Eustorgio (opens in a new tab)

Navigli at a glance

Where
South-west of the centre, around the Darsena and the Naviglio Grande and Naviglio Pavese canals
Getting there
Porta Genova (M2) for the Naviglio Grande, or tram 3 from the Duomo to the Darsena. About 25 minutes on foot from the Duomo.
Best time
Sunset into aperitivo, or the last Sunday of the month for the antiques market
Known for
Canals, aperitivo, vintage shops and nightlife
Name
From the navigli, the navigable canals that once carried the city’s goods
